# Step 5 — Connect the Two Ladders

This is the step where the pile of flat parts becomes a box. Four 13" rails bridge the two ladders — two at the top, two at the bottom — and eight 3" screws lock them in. When you're done you'll have the complete frame, and it will stand up on its own.

It's also the first step that isn't flat, which is why the order below matters more than usual. **Don't try to balance both ladders upright and thread rails into the gap.** Build it lying on its side and let gravity help, exactly like the flat sub-assemblies did.

## What to grab

- [ ] **Both ladders** from Steps 3 and 4
- [ ] **All four 13" rails** — the longer rails, the ones with *no* pocket holes
- [ ] **Wood glue**
- [ ] **3" wood screws** — you'll drive 8
- [ ] **Drill/driver**, plus a **drill bit slightly thinner than the screw shank** for pre-drilling
- [ ] **Tape measure + pencil**
- [ ] **Clamps** — the 16"+ one, plus any others you have
- [ ] **Speed square**, and something long enough to check diagonals (tape measure is fine)
- [ ] **Damp rag**

## Which way the ladders go

Stand each ladder up and turn them so the **pocket-hole faces point at each other**, into the 13" gap. To get there, spin one ladder 180° **about its vertical axis** — never end over end, which would put its 10" rail up near the top.

The ladders are symmetric side to side, so there's no left-handed or right-handed one. Either can be the front. The pocket holes end up hidden inside the finished cabinet, and this step's screw heads end up on the two outer faces — which Step 8 turns to the sides.

## Set the rail heights from your ladders, not from the drawing

The four bottom rails all have to end up in **one flat plane**, because the shelf sits across all four. Same for the top four and the top panel.

- **Top side rails:** flush with the top of the ladders' top rails, which is flush with the leg tops.
- **Bottom side rails:** their **top edge** lines up with the **top edge of the ladders' bottom rails**.

If you marked your 10" line cleanly on both ladders, that top edge is at 13.5" and the nominal numbers work. If a mark drifted a hair, go with what's actually on the wood — measure the built ladder and match it. A rail that's ⅛" off its neighbors makes the shelf rock; a rail that's ⅛" off the drawing but level with its neighbors is invisible.

## Build it lying down

1. **Lay one ladder flat on the floor, pocket-hole face UP.** This is your base for the next few minutes.
2. **Dry-fit the whole thing first, no glue.** Stand the four 13" rails on end in their positions — one at each corner of the ladder, two at the top-frame height and two at the bottom-frame height, each sitting on a leg with its outer edge flush to the outside of the stand. Lower the second ladder on top, pocket face down. Check that everything lines up and the rails aren't proud of the leg edges. Then take the top ladder back off.
3. **Glue the rail ends.** Same as Steps 3 and 4: a thin spread bead on the cut end grain only — the 1½" × 3½" face. Both ends of all four rails.
4. **Stand the rails back in place** on the bottom ladder, then **lower the second ladder onto them.** Nudge until the rail ends sit flush against the legs with no daylight.
5. **Clamp the sandwich together** so nothing shifts while you drill.
6. **Check square before any screw goes in.** Measure the two diagonals across the frame you can see. They should match. If they don't, the box is a parallelogram — push a corner until they do.
7. **Pre-drill and drive the four screws you can reach** — the ones through the upper ladder's outer face. Position and depth below.
8. **Flip the whole assembly over** and repeat for the other four. It's rigid enough to flip once four screws are in.

## Where the screws go

Each screw passes through the **leg's wide (3½") face** — the same face the ladder rails are attached to — and into the end grain of the side rail behind it.

- **¾" in from the leg's outer side edge.** The side rail is 1½" thick and sits flush with the outside of the stand, so ¾" lands you dead center on it.
- **Centered on the rail's height** — about 1¾" down from the top edge of a 3½" rail.
- **Not the narrow 1½" face.** There's no rail behind that one; the screw would come out into open air.

**Pre-drill every one.** A 3" screw driven into end grain near the edge of a 2×4 will split it, and you've got 8 chances to find out. The screw only travels through 1½" of leg before it reaches the rail, so you're not drilling deep.

## Sanity checks

- [ ] **8 screws driven**, one per rail end, none stripped
- [ ] **Top frame diagonals match** — measure corner to corner both ways
- [ ] **Bottom frame diagonals match**
- [ ] **All four bottom rails in one plane** — lay a straightedge across from side to side, both directions, and look for a gap
- [ ] **All four top rails in one plane** — same check; this one carries the tank
- [ ] **It stands on its own** without racking when you push a top corner sideways
- [ ] **No rail proud of a leg's outer edge** — the plywood has to sit flat later
- [ ] **Glue squeeze-out wiped** off all eight joints

## If it racks

Push sideways on a top corner. A little flex now is normal — the plywood top and shelf in Step 6 are what actually stop racking, because a panel screwed across a frame triangulates it. If it still moves noticeably once the panels are on, the fix is a scrap of ¼" plywood tacked across the back, which the build plan mentions under Tips.

## Let it sit

**30 minutes minimum** before you start Step 6, same as the ladders. The frame is a lot heavier and more awkward now, so give the glue its working time rather than hauling it around.

## What's next

You have the finished 3D frame — all 12 2×4 pieces used, 16 screws in. **Step 6** adds the plywood: the top panel the tank sits on, and the notched shelf that drops in around the legs. Steps 6 through 8 are in the [full build plan](stand-build-plan.html).
